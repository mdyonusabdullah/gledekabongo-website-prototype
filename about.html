Meet Gledé

Stories are where secrets come alive.

Gledé Browne Kabongo is an award-winning psychological thriller author, speaker, and strategic communications leader. With more than 20 years of experience in marketing and communications, and a master's degree in communications, she explores the intersection of strategy, storytelling, and influence.

“Where the coverup is more sophisticated than the crime.”

Southwest of Boston

The Author

Addictive psychological thrillers about underestimated women navigating worlds of power, privilege and deadly secrets.

Her nationally recognized psychological thrillers have earned multiple literary awards and finalist honors, including the Silver Falchion Awards, Reader Views Literary Awards, Next Generation Indie Book Awards, the Independent Publisher Book Awards (IPPY), the Eric Hoffer Awards, and the National Indie Excellence Awards.

Drawing on her experience leading strategic communications initiatives for global organizations and writing award-winning fiction, Gledé shares practical insights on storytelling, leadership communication, branding, marketing, creativity, and the psychology behind ideas that move people.

She speaks at conferences, leads workshops and webinars, and serves as Vice President of Sisters in Crime New England.

The Craft

How suspense takes shape.

“The biggest source of suspense for me is character revelation.”

“But it's through the writing process, revising and refining that the suspense really builds.”

01 The Twist

“When I sit down to write, I usually know what the plot twist at the end is going to be; I write out a ten-page outline that includes a few twists and turns.”

02 The Questions

“It comes down to asking a lot of questions, maximizing both internal and external conflicts (can be a goldmine for suspense).”

03 The Stakes

“Making decisions about how to keep raising the stakes, and how to end each chapter to keep readers turning the pages.”

04 The Revelation

“Subplots also have their own ways of adding intrigue and suspense to the story because you never know when a seemingly harmless character might be hiding something sinister.”

On Writing

What are the toughest scenes to write?

“Emotional scenes. As an author, it's my job to be authentic and true to the characters without being over the top. On the other hand, I know if I'm not feeling those emotions after the scene is written, then it won't pull readers in either. It's a delicate balance.” Gledé Browne Kabongo
